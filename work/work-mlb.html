The Challenge

Major League Baseball was facing an identity crisis. The 2022 World Series averaged just 12 million viewers per game — one of the least watched championship series in history. A 2020 Morning Consult study found that only 32% of Gen Z identified as "avid" or "casual" fans. In an increasingly fragmented media landscape, baseball felt like it belonged to a different era.

MLB needed more than advertising. It needed a brand platform — a north star that could speak to the passionate core while opening the door wide enough for a new generation to walk through. Wieden+Kennedy was brought on as creative agency of record, the first in eight years, to build it.

The Insight

Through extensive stakeholder research — interviewing fans across the spectrum from avid to casual, from legacy followers to curious newcomers — a single truth emerged: baseball occupies a completely singular space in American culture. It's nostalgic and new. Slow and explosive. Boring and transcendent. It's a game where a 103 mph fastball and a seven-hour rain delay can exist in the same afternoon.

Nothing else does that. Baseball doesn't need to be defended or modernized. It needs to be held up and seen clearly. Because when you look at it honestly, baseball is just completely, inarguably, something else.

We don't need to make anything up to show the greatness of this game. We're really just holding up a mirror to everything that it is.
— Josh Bogdan, Creative Director, Wieden+Kennedy

Brand Platform: Four Pillars

The strategic work defined MLB's brand attributes — the emotional and functional core that would guide every creative decision across the campaign platform.

Charming
Baseball's quirks aren't bugs — they're features. The seventh-inning stretch, the superstitions, the rituals. No sport has more of them.
Communal
Games are passed down through families. Stadiums are neighborhood landmarks. Baseball is something you inherit and then give to someone else.
Dynamic
The game rewards attention. Every pitch is a decision. The pace isn't slow — it's tense. It's the breath before the swing.
Purposeful
Baseball has always been a mirror for American culture — integrating first, globalizing early, rooting itself in the fabric of cities.

The Strategy

The campaign was deliberately anti-hype. In a media environment saturated with big moments and manufactured drama, "Baseball Is Something Else" leaned into the game's authentic texture — the sounds, the smells, the small rituals that fans have always loved. The approach didn't chase the zeitgeist; it staked out a lane that only baseball could occupy.

Strategy centered on the tension between expected and unexpected — the core creative filter that made the campaign platform scalable across TV, OOH, digital, print, and the MLB Life experiential platform. Every execution had to feel both true to the game's legacy and alive to its present.

Launch timing was critical: the campaign debuted on Opening Day 2023, riding the momentum of the World Baseball Classic — which had already delivered record viewership and renewed global interest in the sport. The goal was to capture that energy and convert it into lasting brand affinity.

By making an investment in our brand, it will make it more clear how to be more welcoming to fans wherever they are in their journey.
— Karin Timpone, Chief Marketing Officer, MLB

The Work

The launch spot — "Overture" — features Cameron J.'s a cappella version of the William Tell Overture laid over a montage of pregame ritual: ticket takers, sizzling hot dogs, grounds crews, batting practice, an Air Force flyover, ending on a Shohei Ohtani pitch. It didn't explain baseball. It made you feel it.

The MLB Life platform extended the campaign beyond advertising — a new content and experiential hub built around baseball's intersection with fashion, food, music, art, and gaming. It gave casual fans a door that didn't require already loving the sport to walk through.

My Role

As Brand Strategist at Wieden+Kennedy Portland, I contributed to the foundational research and strategic development that shaped the "Baseball Is Something Else" platform. The work demanded a deep audit of what made baseball's brand truly distinct — not just from other sports, but as a cultural institution — and how to translate that into a creative platform that could flex across every channel in MLB's ecosystem.

Working alongside brand strategy director Anthony Holton and the creative leadership, I helped examine baseball's audience landscape — the gap between avid fans and casual observers, the Gen Z opportunity, and the global potential being unlocked by the World Baseball Classic. The brief required balancing heritage with growth: honoring the game's legacy while building a genuine onramp for a generation that had grown up without a compelling MLB brand narrative.

The platform we built — grounded in four emotional and functional attributes — became the filter through which every subsequent campaign decision was made. "Baseball Is Something Else" wasn't a tagline. It was a point of view.
